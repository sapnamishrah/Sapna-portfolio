ARC Racer: turning product features into clear consumer creative

My contribution: I designed these creatives and shaped the thinking behind them, across several Elevar shoe launches.

A · Overview

One shoe, a lot to say.

The work covered several Elevar shoes, starting here with ARC Racer, the all-purpose shoe. The creative challenge: communicate its positioning and differentiating features quickly, in a feed, to people who don’t read spec sheets.

The four assets below split that job across formats: introduce the technology, sell the comfort benefit, explain the construction and borrow credibility from an outside reviewer.

B · Creative direction

The four assets

C · Product storytelling

The exploded view

The construction visual labels each part of the shoe so a technical feature becomes something a buyer can point at. Labels as printed in the artwork:

  • Padded tongue
  • TPU heel counter
  • Breathable knit upper
  • Lightweight construction
  • Lockdown lacing system
  • Reinforced eyelets
  • RYDEFOAM cushioned midsole
  • Arch support structure
  • Seamless toe construction
  • Support frame
  • Wide toe box
  • High traction outsole

These are features depicted in the supplied artwork. They have not been independently verified against product documentation.

D · Messaging and positioning

A clear order of ideas

Across the set the hierarchy runs: technology (RYDEFOAM), comfort (the headline benefit), construction (labelled parts), versatility (“all-purpose” and “India’s most advanced all-purpose shoe” recur) and social proof (the reviewer quote).

The review asset quotes a reviewer and shows his video title, “BEST Budget Running Shoe (Elevar ARC Racer)”. The “Tesla of all-purpose shoes” line in asset 3 appears in quotation marks in the artwork.

Also · Blackbird

Same approach, another shoe

The same callout idea works on a dark, studio-lit product shot for Blackbird, with the specs packed into one closing line: “10+ engineering breakthroughs, 1 ultimate all-purpose shoe.”

Labels as printed: soft micro-fiber lining, designed from SR71 Jet, dual heel stabilizer, tilted lacing, breathable mesh, anti-slip lacing, RydeFoam ETPU sole, propulsion arch plate, wide toe box, high traction outsole. As with ARC Racer, these are claims in the artwork, not independently verified.

E · Creative rationale

Different formats, different jobs

Introduce a technology

A single-product hero on a clean set gives the new foam name room to land.

Establish a benefit

A soft, cloud-like scene makes “comfort” visible before the headline is read.

Make features legible

Callouts on an exploded view turn jargon into a quick visual tour.

Another reviewer-led creative lets someone outside the brand make the claim.

No performance, spend or sales data is claimed for these creatives.

Building repeatable growth operations

The work started as an IIM Bangalore live project and became an internship. The challenge: turn manual, fragmented outreach into workflows that are clearer, trackable and repeatable by the next person.

1,000+entries in the participant waitlist (scale of the list, not conversions)
2separate workflows: Scout onboarding and challenge follow-up
1SOP and handover document for the next team member
Pro Senderset up and run until tool and WhatsApp limits stopped it, with the limits recorded
Project 1

Participant waitlist matching

Compared the 1,000+ waitlist against WhatsApp group-member data to find who still needed a reminder. Phone numbers were normalised first so formatting differences did not create false mismatches. Source data was left untouched; comparison happened in a working file.

  1. Participant and waitlist data
  2. Cleaning and phone-number normalisation
  3. Comparison against WhatsApp group members
  4. Classify as joined or pending
  5. Filtered follow-up list
  6. Controlled outreach and status tracking

Illustrative diagram. No participant data shown.

Project 2

Scout recruitment and onboarding

Reviewed Scout profiles (flagging fake ones) and supported the onboarding flow once the team confirmed genuine interest. Reusable badge and email templates kept repeat communication consistent, and a shared sheet showed who owned each status. Group access was only granted after onboarding material went out.

  1. Application review
  2. Interest confirmation and team approval
  3. Badge and onboarding materials
  4. Email communication
  5. WhatsApp follow-up
  6. Status tracking
  7. Group access and later progression

Illustrative diagram of the documented flow.

Project 3

Outreach workflow and documentation

Set up Pro Sender for participant follow-ups and ran it until the tool and WhatsApp restrictions got in the way. It didn’t work as a bulk-sending fix, so the documented approach is small tests first, controlled batches, and stopping to review when restrictions appear. The SOP and knowledge-transfer document record what worked and where it broke, so someone else can continue without verbal context. One mistake is on record too: a wrong link went out in an onboarding email and was corrected the same day.

Outcomes and evidence

What the documents support

  • Worked with a waitlist of 1,000+ entries.
  • Built a more systematic way to find participants who still needed follow-up.
  • Supported a repeatable Scout onboarding workflow, with onboarding-call support.
  • Created reusable badge, email and WhatsApp communication assets.
  • Documented the process in an SOP, including tool limits and lessons.

No conversion rate or onboarding total is claimed. Participant records, screenshots and internal links are intentionally left out for privacy.

“She Always Knew How to Save”

A Mother’s Day campaign concept built for UTI SIP, developed while working directly under the VP and EVP of Marketing.

Concept

The gullak and the rice dabba

Many Indian mothers quietly built savings in the gullak or the rice dabba. The campaign uses that familiar habit as a metaphor for the discipline behind a SIP, so the product feels like something people already understand from home.
